## Formal boundaries and scope

- The full all-dimensional interval theorem is formalized by IV01.twoCheckerVertices. It quantifies over arbitrary real entries and all prescribed signatures, allows zero proper minors and fixed entries of both parities, and concludes nonsingularity as well as every weak minor sign.
- Paper Lemma 3.1 also states inverse nonnegativity throughout an inverse-positive interval. The cited isolated Lean result proves nonsingularity only. This difference does not add an assumption to the main declaration.
- Paper Lemma 5.2 has a bound depending on the actual deficient rank t. The isolated Lean bound uses p in both factors, which suffices for the two forbidden sizes used by the complete proof.
- The target states the checker-vertex-to-box implication. Its converse follows by the two proved checker-vertex membership facts. Zero-based matrix indices preserve checker parity when both indices are shifted to the paper convention.
- The paper examples and their all-dimensional direct-sum explanations are not separate Lean theorems. The rational programs check the stated finite instances; the universal theorem is supplied independently by the full Lean proof.
- This correspondence checker verifies references and the complete numbered-result inventory. It does not compile Lean or establish mathematical equivalence from prose; use the separate build and axiom commands in VERIFICATION.md.
- The theorem concerns real square entrywise Cartesian boxes and one common signature at nonsingular checker endpoints. It gives no unconditional singular-endpoint theorem, no replacement by the ordinary lower and upper bounds, and no polynomial-time claim for evaluating all required endpoint minors.
